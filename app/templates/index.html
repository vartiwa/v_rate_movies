Be Suspicious of Online Movie Ratings, Especially Fandango’s

In 2015, Walt Hickey investigated Fandango's 5-star movie rating system and uncovered a systematic upward bias: displayed stars were consistently higher than the actual ratings hidden in the page's HTML code. Below is an interactive replication of the 2015 investigation, cross-platform critic benchmarks, the commercial incentives at play, and an audit of post-article 2016–2017 data.

Why This Matters

When an entertainment consumer visits Rotten Tomatoes or Metacritic, the site's primary business model is content consumption and advertising. When an entertainment consumer visits Fandango, the website's primary revenue stream is a per-ticket convenience fee. An asymmetric rounding rule that ensured zero films fell below 3.0 stars and pushed 89% of releases to 4.0–5.0 stars served as a potent psychological conversion optimizer.

Chronology of the Fandango Investigation

Key historical milestones from initial reporting to site remediation

October 15, 2015

Walt Hickey Publishes FiveThirtyEight Investigation

Hickey scrapes 510 films and 146 major releases, proving that Fandango’s displayed star ratings were systematically higher than the actual ratings in the page HTML.

October 16, 2015

Fandango Acknowledges Display Code "Glitch"

Fandango responds publicly, stating: "We found a software bug in our rounding algorithm and are immediately working on a fix to ensure displayed stars reflect exact customer scores."

2016 – 2017

Independent Follow-up Study (Alex Olteanu)

Analysis of 214 films confirms displayed star ratings dropped by ~0.20 stars (4.09 → 3.89), aligning closely with 2015's true HTML baseline (3.85).

2019 – Present

Transition to Verified Audience Scores

Fandango acquired Rotten Tomatoes and eventually phased out its proprietary 5-star fan meter in favor of the Verified Tomato Audience Meter.

Frequently Asked Questions & Methodology Notes

Common questions regarding the datasets, scraping, and statistical validity

How was the true HTML rating extracted?
In 2015, Fandango's web pages contained an underlying numeric value inside HTML attributes (e.g. <div class="star-rating" data-rating="4.1">) while simultaneously rendering 4.5 visual star images via CSS sprites. Hickey parsed the unrounded value from the raw HTML.
Why did the dataset filter for movies with ≥30 fan votes?
To eliminate obscure films with insufficient sample sizes where a single fan vote could drastically swing averages. The 30-vote threshold isolated active theatrical releases.
Was the 2016–2017 drop statistically significant?
Yes. Welch's two-sample \(t\)-test yielded \(t = 3.38\) (\(p = 0.0008\)) and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test yielded \(D = 0.201\) (\(p = 0.0003\)), confirming that displayed star ratings were significantly lower post-investigation.
